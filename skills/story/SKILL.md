# Skill: story

## Purpose
Turn a premise, research package, or informational goal into a coherent narrative/expository structure.

## Inputs
- project intent
- audience
- fact ledger when applicable
- target duration/profile
- genre/form
- constraints

## Outputs
- premise
- logline or core question
- theme
- viewer promise
- beginning/middle/end logic
- act/chapter map
- sequence cards
- scene cards
- narration/dialogue draft
- setup/payoff ledger

## Story tests
Every unit should state at least one of:
- what changes
- what is learned
- what is decided
- what is gained/lost
- what tension increases/releases
- what mechanism becomes understandable

## Expository structure
Prefer:
question → model → evidence → mechanism → implication → conclusion

Use alternatives when the subject needs chronology, comparison, process, case study, investigation, or argument.

## Dramatic structure
Track:
- objective
- obstacle
- tactic
- turn
- consequence
- new question

## Long-form rule
Do not draft hundreds of scenes before the sequence structure is stable.
Lock sequence cards, then expand locally.

## Gate
Fail if:
- scene has no function,
- sequence does not advance the audience contract,
- factual narration exceeds evidence,
- setup/payoff is broken,
- conclusion does not answer the promised question.
